# Keep reef rubble from moving

Research reviewed October 23, 2026. AI-assisted family background research, not a student-authored fair submission. No prototype, experimental outcome or global novelty claim has been validated.

**Status: higher-risk reserve, not a current lead. All-in planning total $1,055.** The physical apparatus is plausible; the case for substantial ML value and biological transfer is weaker than for a direct pollutant-removal endpoint.


**Plain problem:** After damage, loose coral skeleton pieces can keep shifting rather than providing a stable foundation for recovery. The instability itself is a measurable physical bottleneck. This project restores the substrate; it does not anchor transplanted seagrass or invent an artificial reef ornament. [Primary rubble-motion study, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coastaleng.2025.104931).

**Intervention:** Equal-mass rubble mounds held initially by low-material, open coir mesh arrangements. Optimize aperture relative to piece size, mound footprint/aspect, and tying pattern, seeking low movement while leaving a large stable surface uncovered. Test both intact support and a deliberately standardized **support-removal challenge**. Removal is a mechanical stress test, not accelerated proof of marine biodegradation.

**Why this is not simply solved:** Coir reef bags, mesh grids and steel reef stars are established restoration tools. A 2025 field trial found improved physical binding/stability at some sites but no significant coral recruitment improvement; mesh aperture and site conditions matter. [Kenyon et al., Ecological Engineering 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoleng.2024.107433). This sharply limits claims that stable rubble automatically restores corals. An August 2026 **preprint**, not yet treated here as peer-reviewed, found mound mass and rubble branching may matter more than mesh presence. [Kenyon et al. preprint](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7275154). Consequently fix total mass rather than present a bigger, heavier mound as a clever new design.

**Closest programs/products:** [Great Barrier Reef Authority Project Reefresh](https://www.gbrmpa.gov.au/our-work/field-management/project-reefresh-bait-reef-rehabilitation) directly compares bags, mounds and control rubble; [MARRS](https://www.buildingcoral.com/our-solution) already deploys reef stars. A 2025 Bayesian decision-support paper already selects among restoration methods using global evidence, so “AI chooses a rubble method” is not new. [Leung et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2025.128154). A targeted search of ISEF abstracts did not establish an exact student duplicate, but this is not evidence of absence.

**Narrow possible contribution:** A physically validated equal-material design procedure that preserves *open stable surface area* across mixed disturbance sequences and after support removal, beating a conventional bag and a matched unbagged mound. No broad claim that coir bags, packing or biodegradable mesh are new. This gap remains provisional and vulnerable to the very recent literature.

**Hardware:** Contained paddle tank; removable bed cassette; overhead camera with fixed scale; reusable uncollected terrestrial limestone fragments from one batch; coir mesh; ruler/point-depth gauge; traps for all escaped pieces/fibres. Use the printer for cutting/assembly jigs and controlled comparison forms. Do not collect coral, add PLA to the sea, culture unknown organisms or make a biodegradable-plastic claim. Angular limestone is a model substrate, not a representative sample of branching coral skeleton.

**ML:** Constrained Gaussian-process design optimization or a small ensemble surrogate predicts moved mass and open stable area from the three design variables plus measured wave descriptors and substrate size/shape summaries. Use original physical intervention results, not public photographs as action labels. Compare an equal-experiment-budget quadratic response surface and a predeclared factorial/random search, alongside conventional structures. Model design must be frozen before final confirmation. If simple aperture-to-piece-size rules explain essentially all performance, ML does not meet the user's requirement.

**Validation:** Approximately 24–30 development configurations, physically reset, then two final designs plus conventional bag, equal-mass unbagged mound and flat loose rubble on withheld disturbance blocks. The two final designs must include the learned selection and the strongest conventional search/model selection if they differ. A starting confirmation allocation is five treatments × three withheld disturbance blocks × four freshly prepared mound replicates = 60 runs, for 84–90 runs including development. Four replicates per cell is a planning count, not a power calculation; verify labor, consumables and sensitivity using the pilot. Randomize position/day and re-pack independent replicates; do not count tracked stones or camera frames as independent mounds. Measure escaped/moved dry mass, mound subsidence, marked-surface movement, exposed surface fraction and fibre loss. Blind manual image-grid scoring should check automated area estimates. State clearly that plan-view openness is not true 3-D settlement area, larval settlement, coral survival or ecological recruitment.

**Fair test:** Same material mass, initial substrate batch, occupied area constraint and cumulative wave forcing. A dense sealed bag should not win by covering every surface. Require both stability and minimum accessible open area, and count loss into surrounding habitat as failure rather than movement out of the camera view. A separate unbagged mound baseline prevents crediting ordinary piling to ML.

**Pros:** Direct restoration aim; simple physical endpoint; no access to reefs or live corals needed for the core engineering experiment; no exotic chemistry. **Cons:** Exact environmental benefit remains a physical proxy; substantial recent prior art; tank/wave scale and angular model rubble constrain transfer. **Mitigation:** Present it as material-efficient substrate stabilization, validate with unbagged mounds and support-removal trials, publish the full failure envelope and retain every fibre in the tank. Do not claim improved recruitment or marine biodegradability. **Novelty/ML risk:** High enough that this is a reserve, not an automatic top recommendation.

**Go/no-go:** In the first two approved weeks, require measurable rubble motion at repeatable forcing, no tank reflection/whole-bed failure dominating every run, area-scoring agreement, and a real stability/openness tradeoff. Stop if small changes do not matter, if extra mass explains the gain, if the best simple bag/mound dominates, or if a surrogate cannot improve design selection against equal-budget classical search. Ten-week contained testing is plausible; biological reef recovery is not.


**Avoid a false support-removal result:** use a standardized release/cut-and-lift procedure that minimizes direct stone displacement, and matched sham handling where support is retained. Count movement caused by the procedure and report it separately from later wave movement. This cannot reproduce gradual biodegradation or biological binding. Freshly prepare each independent mound, block by reused stone batch, log fracture/wear, and replace damaged material consistently. The camera must observe a wave-height reference; check wave amplitude and reflection/reproducibility before confirmation. Report visible/captured fibre loss at the stated collection-mesh size and balance detection limit, not an unmeasured claim of zero fibre shedding.
