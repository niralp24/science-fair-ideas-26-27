### Preapproval, chemicals, fabrication and water/electricity

The local [SRC instructions](https://science-fair.org/rules-and-registration/src/) explicitly require preapproval and Form 3 for **all projects using chemicals**, and for hazardous activities/devices. They also require preapproval for projects using lasers of any power. Experimental work requiring preapproval starts only after the required SRC authorization; official pages use differing status labels, so follow the SRC instructions for the actual project. Changes after approval require further review before proceeding. All former participants in this or another ISEF-affiliated fair must submit for preapproval; the SRC decides continuation status.

**Application to this family:** disclose laser-cutter fabrication and the actual chemical/sensor consumables. Do not assume an enclosed cutter or a dilute salt solution creates an exemption. The local page does not provide a categorical exemption for a low-voltage water rig. Conversely, it does not say that every pump or every water experiment is automatically hazardous; classification depends on the actual setup.

Under the [ISEF hazardous-activities rules](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/hazardous-chemicals-activities-or-devices/), assess relevant risks with the supervisor before experimentation, document Form 3, use appropriate supervision, read chemical SDSs, and specify handling, ventilation and disposal. Chemical-use equipment cannot return to household use. Materials whose SDS identifies carcinogenic, teratogenic or reproductive hazards require the specified institutional/restricted-fume-hood setting. A protected low-voltage DC rig, guarded moving parts, separated electronics, suitable electrical protection and spill containment are sensible design precautions; they are not a substitute for SRC classification.

### Original-data ML and engineering

The [minimum-quality requirements](https://science-fair.org/rules-and-registration/project-standards/) recognize engineering as designing, building and testing a solution. A high-school engineering project needs at least three measurable design criteria, constraints, materials, relevant diagrams/algorithms, fabrication details, and a stepwise test plan for each criterion. Tests must be repeated at least three times; that minimum does not establish adequate statistical power.

The additional Data Analysis/AI requirements are expressly framed around mining data the student **did not generate**. They include traceable sources, justified model choice, curation, separate validation data and at least two of five performance areas. This does not establish a ban on ML trained on original apparatus experiments. For such a hardware project, separate development and frozen-model confirmation runs remain good scientific practice; do not describe every additional data-mining requirement as automatically mandatory for original-data ML. The project must be an engineering contribution, rather than high-school product comparison alone.

### Biological incubations are a real selection gate

The [current ISEF biological rules](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/potentially-hazardous-biological-agents/) prohibit culturing potentially hazardous agents at home, including BSL-1 organisms. BSL-1 work requires an appropriate laboratory and supervision; opening an unknown-microorganism culture invokes BSL-2 precautions. There is an exemption for water/soil microbes **not concentrated in growth-conducive media**, with Form 3 still required. Local requirements can be stricter. Students may not collect HAB water; qualified collection and BSL-2 testing are specified.

Therefore neither every natural-water test nor every sediment sample is automatically prohibited. However, deliberately enriching organic effluent, feeding unknown microbes, or keeping nutrient-rich sediment incubations cannot simply be declared a harmless home-water experiment. Have the SRC assess the actual protocol. There is no verified permission here for home silage decomposition, unknown-culture BOD experiments or enriched lake-sediment release chambers.

### Student ownership, generative AI and the ML mentor

The [2027 changes page](https://science-fair.org/2027-changes-from-previous-rules-procedures/) states that AI assistance must be acknowledged and presented material must be the student's own work. It prohibits generative AI writing the research plan, abstract or poster, and generating citations. It also requires a notebook at check-in and judging; a digital notebook needs an offline complete copy and printed summary. Form 2A replaces the previous assistance-disclosure form. A substantially changed project after initial review can require renewed review or a new application.

The [linked Society AI-use table](https://sspcdn.blob.core.windows.net/files/Documents/SEP/ISEF/2026/Rules/Generative-AI-Use-Table.pdf) allows initial code assistance with explicit identification of AI-generated portions and a prompt log. Idea development can use AI with the stated logging/acknowledgment conditions. It forbids AI-generated conclusions and a starter bibliography. These family notes and linked sources must not be copied into a student plan or submitted bibliography: the student must independently engage with the sources and author the submission.

The local [RRI definition](https://science-fair.org/rules-and-registration/registered-research-institution-use/) includes professional research scientists/engineers providing advice, and substantial guidance from nonteacher mentors. Home construction alone does not establish non-RRI status. Disclose what the ML mentor actually contributes; the fair determines classification. [ISEF documentation rules](https://www.societyforscience.org/isef/international-rules/rules-for-all-projects/) require delineating student versus mentor work and the applicable support/institution forms, including virtual institutional mentoring where relevant.

### What can be shown on fair day

The local [display rules](https://science-fair.org/students-parents/project-display-rules/) prohibit liquids, including water; raw soil/sand/waste samples; glass; and live organisms. A hardware prototype is optional and must fit within 15 by 24 inches. Use a dry safe prototype, photographs, recorded experiments and data. The page also requires guarded moving parts and protected electrical connections, restricts power access, and bars exposed live circuits above 36 V. Its laser-display wording differs from the SRC page's blanket statement; no laser display is needed for these projects, and none should be assumed permissible from this memo.
